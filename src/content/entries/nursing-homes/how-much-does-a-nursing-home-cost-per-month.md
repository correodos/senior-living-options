> **Key Takeaways**
>
> - The national median cost of a nursing home is **$9,581 per month** for a semi-private room and **$10,798 per month** for a private room (2025).
> - That is **$315 and $355 per day**, or **$114,975 and $129,575 per year** (2025). Prices rose 2% and 1% from 2024.
> - Where you live changes the bill a lot. A semi-private room has a median of **$5,627 per month** in Texas and **$27,831 per month** in Alaska (2025).
> - A private room costs about **$1,217 more per month** than a semi-private room at the national median (our calculation from 2025 figures).
> - Medicare pays for a nursing home only in the short term. After day 100 of skilled care, you pay all costs. Medicaid is the primary payer for **63%** of nursing facility residents (2025).

---

You are probably looking at a nursing home bill, or a price sheet, and trying to understand whether the number is normal. It is a hard moment. The figures are large, and every facility seems to explain them differently.

This article gives you the national and state medians for what a nursing home costs per month, so you have a fair yardstick. You will see the difference between a semi-private and a private room, what a stay costs over several months, how nursing homes compare with other types of care, and who helps pay. All cost figures come from the 2025 CareScout Cost of Care Survey, the most recent edition published. Your own price may differ, so use these as a starting point and ask each facility for a written fee schedule.

## How Much Does a Nursing Home Cost Per Month? The National Median

The 2025 CareScout Cost of Care Survey (published March 2026) reports these national medians for a nursing home. A median means half of the prices were higher and half were lower.

| Room type | Daily | Monthly | Annual |
| --------- | ----- | ------- | ------ |
| **Semi-private room** | $315 | $9,581 | $114,975 |
| **Private room** | $355 | $10,798 | $129,575 |

Compared with 2024, the semi-private median rose **2%** and the private median rose **1%**. The press release links the modest increases to ongoing inflation and workforce pressures in higher-acuity care settings.

The monthly figure equals the annual figure divided by 12. A $315 daily rate over 365 days gives $114,975 a year, or about $9,581 a month.

*Note: The survey collected prices from July to November 2025 and gathered more than 25,000 rates. The press release does not say which services each rate includes, so ask every facility what its daily rate covers.*

## Semi-Private vs Private Room: What the Difference Costs

A semi-private room is shared with another resident. A private room is yours alone. At the national median, the private room costs more in every way:

| Difference at the national median (2025) | Amount |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------ |
| **Per day** | $40 |
| **Per month** | $1,217 |
| **Per year** | $14,600 |

These differences are our calculations from the CareScout medians above.

Medicare's list of covered skilled nursing facility services names a **semi-private room**. A private room is not named on that list. So if your loved one qualifies for Medicare's short-term coverage and wants a private room, ask the facility in advance who pays the difference.

## What a Nursing Home Costs Over Time

Families often ask what a whole stay will cost. A monthly median makes it easy to estimate. These are our calculations, not survey results, and they assume the price stays the same:

| Length of stay | Semi-private room | Private room |
| -------------- | ----------------- | ------------ |
| **1 month** | $9,581 | $10,798 |
| **3 months** | $28,743 | $32,394 |
| **6 months** | $57,486 | $64,788 |
| **1 year** | $114,975 | $129,575 |

Real stays are rarely this tidy. Prices usually go up from year to year, and a person's care needs can change. For a short rehab stay after a hospital visit, Medicare may cover part of the cost. The section "Who Pays for a Nursing Home" explains how.

## Nursing Home Cost Per Month by State

Location is the biggest factor you cannot control. CareScout collects prices by metropolitan area and then reports a median for each state. Here is a sample of the 2025 state medians:

| State | Semi-private (monthly) | Private (monthly) |
| ----- | ---------------------- | ----------------- |
| **Texas** | $5,627 | $7,604 |
| **Missouri** | $6,741 | $7,604 |
| **Illinois** | $8,304 | $9,216 |
| **Ohio** | $9,186 | $10,389 |
| **Florida** | $10,342 | $12,167 |
| **Pennsylvania** | $11,954 | $13,688 |
| **California** | $12,167 | $15,178 |
| **Massachusetts** | $14,448 | $15,817 |
| **New York** | $15,528 | $16,729 |
| **Oregon** | $16,760 | $18,448 |
| **Alaska** | $27,831 | Not enough data |

Alaska's semi-private median is almost **five times** Texas's (our calculation). Oregon has the highest private-room median in the survey, at $18,448 per month. CareScout did not have enough data to report an Alaska private-room price.

CareScout publishes costs for 434 regions, based on metropolitan areas, so prices can also differ inside one state. See [nursing home costs for all 50 states](/costs/) to find your state's median, and use the CareScout cost tool at carescout.com/cost-of-care for local estimates.

## Nursing Home Cost Per Month vs Other Types of Care

Among the options in the survey, a nursing home costs the most. Here are the 2025 national medians side by side:

| Type of care | Monthly cost (2025) |
| ------------ | ------------------- |
| **Adult day health care, 5 days a week** | $2,058 |
| **Assisted living, private one-bedroom** | $6,200 |
| **In-home care, 44 hours a week** | $6,673 |
| **Nursing home, semi-private room** | $9,581 |
| **Nursing home, private room** | $10,798 |

These options are not interchangeable. Assisted living helps with daily tasks, while a nursing home provides skilled nursing care. For help choosing, see our guides to [assisted living](/article/assisted-living/assisted-living-complete-guide/) and [in-home care costs](/article/in-home-care/how-much-does-in-home-care-cost-per-month/).

## Who Pays for a Nursing Home Each Month

Most residents have a public program as their primary payer. Here is how the main sources of payment work.

### Medicare: Short-Term Only

Medicare Part A pays for skilled nursing facility care for up to **100 days** per benefit period, after a qualifying hospital stay. In 2026, days 1-20 cost **$0** after the **$1,736** deductible, and days 21-100 cost **$217 per day**. After day 100, you pay all costs. Medicare also says it does not cover custodial care when that is the only care you need. Our article [Does Medicare Pay for Nursing Home Care?](/article/nursing-homes/does-medicare-pay-for-nursing-home-care/) explains every rule.

### Medicaid: The Largest Payer

Medicaid may cover long-term nursing home care if you meet your state's requirements. It is the primary payer for **63%** of nursing facility residents, while Medicare is the primary payer for 14% and another source, such as out-of-pocket payment, for 23% (2025). To qualify, most people must have income and savings below their state's limits. Our article on [Medicaid spend-down](/article/senior-care-costs/what-is-medicaid-spend-down/) explains how that works.

### Long-Term Care Insurance and Other Sources

A private long-term care insurance policy may pay part of the monthly bill, up to its daily or monthly limit. Check the waiting period and benefit limit before you rely on it. Our article on [whether long-term care insurance is worth it](/article/senior-care-costs/long-term-care-insurance-worth-it/) explains how benefits work. Some veterans and surviving spouses may qualify for the VA's Aid and Attendance pension. Our [nursing home guide](/article/nursing-homes/nursing-homes-complete-guide/) lists the current VA amounts.

### Paying Privately

If no program applies, the family pays the monthly rate directly. Many people start this way and then apply for Medicaid when their savings run low. Ask every facility whether it accepts Medicaid before admission, so a later change in payment does not force a move.

## Questions to Ask Before You Sign

The same monthly rate can cover different things at different facilities. Ask each one:

1. What exactly does the daily rate include?
2. Which services or supplies cost extra, and how much?
3. How much notice do you give before raising the rate?
4. Do you accept Medicaid, and what happens if my loved one switches to it?
5. Will you put the rate and terms in writing?

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does a nursing home cost per year?

The 2025 national median is **$114,975 per year** for a semi-private room and **$129,575 per year** for a private room. These figures equal the daily medians of $315 and $355 over 365 days.

### How much does a nursing home cost per day?

The 2025 national median is **$315 per day** for a semi-private room and **$355 per day** for a private room, up 2% and 1% from 2024. Daily prices vary widely by state.

### Which state has the cheapest nursing homes?

In the 2025 survey, Texas has the lowest semi-private median at **$5,627 per month**. Missouri follows at $6,741 per month. Alaska has the highest at **$27,831 per month**.

### How much more does a private room cost than a semi-private room?

At the 2025 national medians, a private room costs about **$1,217 more per month**, or $14,600 more per year (our calculation). Medicare's skilled nursing facility coverage lists a semi-private room, so ask who pays the difference.

### Is a nursing home more expensive than assisted living?

Yes. The 2025 national median for a semi-private nursing home room is **$9,581 per month**, compared with **$6,200 per month** for assisted living. The care is different: nursing homes provide skilled nursing care.
