> **Key Takeaways**
>
> - Medicaid is the main payer for long-term nursing home care. It is the primary payer for **63%** of nursing facility residents (2025, KFF).
> - To qualify, a person must pass three tests: a **medical need** for nursing home care, **assets** under the state's limit, and **income** within the state's rules.
> - The federal ceiling for the special nursing home income level is **$2,982 per month** (2026). Above it, some states use a qualified income trust or a medical-bills spend-down.
> - Once Medicaid pays, the resident's income, after small allowances, goes to the facility. The resident keeps a **personal needs allowance** of at least **$30 per month**.
> - Apply early. States have **45 days** to decide most applications, and the back-pay window shrinks from 3 months to **2 months** for applications filed from January 1, 2027.

Your parent needs a nursing home, and the monthly bill is more than the family can pay for long. Someone mentions Medicaid, and you are not sure what it covers, who qualifies or how to start. That is a stressful place to be, and a common one.

This article explains how Medicaid pays for a nursing home: what it covers, the three eligibility tests, how income limits work, how much the resident keeps, and how to apply. For the asset rules in detail, such as the 5-year look-back and what a spouse at home can keep, we link to our guide on [Medicaid spend-down](/article/senior-care-costs/what-is-medicaid-spend-down/). Rules differ by state, so confirm details with your state Medicaid agency.

## Does Medicaid Pay for Nursing Homes?

Yes, for people who qualify. Medicare pays only for short stays, up to 100 days of skilled care after a hospital stay. Medicaid is the program that pays for long-term care. See [Does Medicare Pay for Nursing Home Care?](/article/nursing-homes/does-medicare-pay-for-nursing-home-care/) for the Medicare side.

Here is what the rules say about Medicaid and nursing facilities:

- **It is a required benefit.** States must cover nursing facility services for adults age 21 or older who need them. States cannot keep a waiting list for it.
- **It pays last.** Medicaid nursing facility services are available only when other payment options are not.
- **It works only in certified homes.** The nursing home must be licensed and certified as a Medicaid nursing facility. If it is not, the resident would have to move to get coverage.
- **It is common.** About **1.3 million people** used Medicaid nursing facility services in 2023.

Some items are not included. A private room (unless medically needed), personal clothing, and a phone or television can be charged to the resident.

*Note: "Most, but not all, nursing homes accept Medicaid payment," according to Medicare. Ask before you choose a home.*

## Medicaid Nursing Home Eligibility: The Three Tests

Eligibility is "most often" based on income and personal resources, and the rules differ in every state. You must also live in the state where you apply. For nursing home coverage, three tests matter.

| Test | What the state checks |
| ---- | --------------------- |
| **Medical need** | Does the person need a nursing home level of care? |
| **Assets** | Are countable assets under the state's limit? |
| **Income** | Is monthly income within the state's limit or method? |

### Test 1: Medical Need (Level of Care)

Medicaid does not pay for a nursing home just because a person is low-income. States use "level of care" criteria that look at what a person can do, not at a diagnosis. They use assessment tools to measure it.

A person with a serious mental illness or an intellectual disability must also be screened before admission to decide whether nursing facility care is needed and appropriate. This screening is called PASRR.

### Test 2: Assets

The federal SSI resource standard is **$2,000** for one person (2026, CMS). Many states use that figure for nursing home Medicaid, but not all do. Two examples from state agencies:

| State | Asset limit, single person |
| ----- | -------------------------- |
| **Connecticut** | $1,600 |
| **Rhode Island** | $4,000 |

*Check your own state in our [costs by state guide](/costs/).*

The home itself is not counted as an asset, but Medicaid will not pay for long-term care if home equity is above a limit. In 2026 the minimum limit is **$752,000** and the maximum is **$1,130,000**, and each state chooses within that range. Starting January 1, 2028, a new federal law caps the maximum at **$1,000,000** for homes not on agricultural land.

Gifts and transfers count too. Medicaid looks back at past gifts, and a gift can delay coverage. Read [What Is Medicaid Spend-Down?](/article/senior-care-costs/what-is-medicaid-spend-down/) before moving any money.

The third test, income, has its own section below.

## Medicaid Nursing Home Income Limits

States may cover nursing home residents with higher income than other Medicaid groups. The federal limit for this "special income level" is **300% of the SSI federal benefit rate**. In 2026, the benefit rate is **$994 per month**, so the ceiling is **$2,982 per month**. Medicare says many states set higher income limits for nursing home residents.

What happens when income is above the limit depends on the state. There are two main paths:

- **Special income level states.** Some of these states use a **qualified income trust**, also called a Miller trust. Funds placed in the trust may be used to pay the cost of the person's care, up to an amount the state sets.
- **Medically needy states.** These states let a person subtract medical bills from income until it falls to the state's medically needy level. This is the income form of spend-down.

These two paths are generally found in different states. Ask your state agency which one applies, and see an elder law attorney before you create a trust.

*Note: The $2,982 figure is a federal maximum, not a promise. Your state may set a different limit or use another method.*

## How Much Does the Resident Keep?

Qualifying for Medicaid does not mean the resident pays nothing. Federal rules say that income left after set deductions reduces what the state pays the nursing home. Everything left after the deductions below goes toward the bill, and Medicaid pays the rest.

The deductions come out of income in this order:

1. **A personal needs allowance.** It must be at least **$30 per month** for an aged, blind or disabled person. It covers clothing and other personal needs.
2. **An allowance for a spouse at home.** This helps the spouse who is not in the nursing home.
3. **An allowance for other family at home.** It depends on the number of family members.
4. **Medical bills not paid by someone else.** This includes health insurance premiums and out-of-pocket costs.

States set the personal needs allowance above the federal minimum if they choose. As of July 2026 it ranges from **$30 to $200 per month**, depending on the state.

## How to Apply for Medicaid for a Nursing Home

You apply through the Medicaid program in the state where the person lives. Medicare points people to their State Medical Assistance (Medicaid) office. Here is a practical order of steps:

1. **Check that the home takes Medicaid.** Ask whether the home is Medicaid-certified, whether it has a Medicaid bed, and what happens when private money runs out.
2. **Ask the state agency for its checklist.** Each state lists the documents it needs. Gather bank statements going back several years, because of the look-back.
3. **Submit the application and answer every request.** The agency can take longer when an applicant is slow to respond, so reply quickly and keep copies.
4. **Wait for the decision.** States must decide within **45 days** for most applicants, and within **90 days** when the application is based on disability. Delays are allowed in some cases, such as when the applicant or a physician is slow to respond.

### Back-Pay and the 2027 Change

Today, Medicaid can pay for care received up to **3 months before the month of application**, if the person would have been eligible then. For applications made on or after **January 1, 2027**, that window becomes **2 months** for people outside the Medicaid expansion adult group. That is one more reason not to wait.

### What the Law Says the Nursing Home Cannot Do

Federal rules protect residents who use or will use Medicaid. A nursing home cannot require a third party, such as a son or daughter, to guarantee payment. It also cannot ask for a gift or donation as a condition of admission or of staying.

A home can discharge a resident for non-payment, and that includes not turning in the paperwork needed for a third-party payer. The home must give at least **30 days' written notice** in most cases. Keep Medicaid paperwork up to date.

## What to Do While the Application Is Pending

The bills do not stop during the wait. In 2025, the national median cost was **$9,581 per month** for a semi-private room and **$10,798 per month** for a private room (our monthly calculation from the survey's daily rates). See [how much a nursing home costs per month](/article/nursing-homes/how-much-does-a-nursing-home-cost-per-month/) and [costs in your state](/costs/).

While you wait:

- Ask the home in writing how it bills while Medicaid is pending, and keep every receipt.
- Check whether the person has other coverage, such as [long-term care insurance](/article/senior-care-costs/long-term-care-insurance-worth-it/).

If the person does not need a nursing home level of care yet, state waiver programs may pay for care at home. Read our [guide to in-home care](/article/in-home-care/in-home-care-complete-guide/).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does Medicaid pay for nursing home care?

Yes, for people who meet the state's rules. States must cover nursing facility care for adults age 21 and older who need it, and Medicaid is the primary payer for 63% of nursing facility residents (2025). The home must be Medicaid-certified, and Medicaid pays only after other payment sources.

### What is the income limit for Medicaid nursing home care?

The federal ceiling for the special nursing home income level is $2,982 per month in 2026, which is 300% of the SSI federal benefit rate of $994. Your state may use a different limit or method. If income is higher, some states require a qualified income trust and others let medical bills lower countable income.

### How much money can you have and still qualify for Medicaid in a nursing home?

The federal SSI resource standard is $2,000 for one person in 2026, and many states use it. Others differ: Rhode Island allows $4,000, while Connecticut uses $1,600. Your home, up to your state's equity limit, is generally not counted.

### How much of my income do I keep in a nursing home on Medicaid?

You keep a personal needs allowance of at least $30 per month. States can set a higher amount, and as of July 2026 amounts range from $30 to $200. After allowances for a spouse or family at home and certain medical bills, the rest of your income goes toward the cost of care.

### How long does it take to get approved for Medicaid for a nursing home?

States have 45 days to decide most applications and 90 days when the application is based on disability. Delays are allowed in some situations, such as missing paperwork. If you are approved, Medicaid can pay for care received up to 3 months before the application month for applications filed before 2027, and 2 months after that.

### Can a nursing home refuse to take Medicaid?

Yes. Most nursing homes accept Medicaid, but not all, and only a Medicaid-certified home can be paid by Medicaid. A home that accepts Medicaid cannot ask a family member to guarantee payment or require a donation as a condition of admission.
