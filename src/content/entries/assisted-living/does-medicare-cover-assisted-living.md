> **Key Takeaways**
>
> - Medicare does not pay for assisted living. Medicare says it "doesn't pay for long-term care," and that includes help with daily tasks in an assisted living facility.
> - Medicare can still pay for medical care a resident receives, such as doctor visits, outpatient therapy and, under certain conditions, home health care and hospice.
> - After a qualifying 3-day hospital stay, Medicare covers up to **100 days** of skilled nursing facility care. In 2026, days 21-100 cost **$217 per day**.
> - Assisted living costs a national median of **$6,200 per month** (2025), so the gap Medicare leaves is large.
> - Medicaid, PACE, VA benefits and long-term care insurance are the programs that can help with the rest.

---

You are probably asking this question because someone you love needs more help than they can get at home, and you are trying to work out who will pay. Most families assume Medicare will. It is the insurance most older adults have, and it covers a lot. Finding out that it does not pay for assisted living can be a shock.

This article gives you the plain answer to "does Medicare cover assisted living," and then goes further. You will see exactly what Medicare does pay for when a loved one lives in assisted living, where families get caught out, and which programs can help with the bill instead. Every rule here comes from official sources: Medicare.gov, the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) and Medicaid.gov.

## Does Medicare Cover Assisted Living? The Short Answer

No. Original Medicare does not pay for assisted living. That means it does not pay the monthly fee, which covers the apartment, meals, housekeeping and daily personal care.

What Medicare does pay for is medical care. The split looks like this:

| What you need | Does Medicare pay? |
| ------------- | ------------------ |
| **Rent and meals** | No |
| **Help with bathing and dressing** | No, if it is the only care needed |
| **Doctor visits** | Yes, under Part B |
| **Outpatient therapy** | Yes, if medically necessary |
| **Home health care** | Sometimes, if conditions are met |
| **Hospice care** | Yes, but not room and board |
| **Short-term rehab after a hospital stay** | Yes, up to 100 days |

The rest of this article explains each row.

## Why Medicare Doesn't Pay for Assisted Living

Medicare is health insurance. It pays for medical treatment. Assisted living mostly provides something else: help with everyday life.

Medicare describes long-term care as mostly non-medical, and it includes **custodial care**: help with daily personal tasks such as bathing, dressing and eating. Medicare says it does not pay for long-term care, and it names assisted living as one of the places where people get it. You pay all costs for services Medicare does not cover.

Medigap, the supplement some people buy to fill Medicare's gaps, does not change this. Medicare says Medicare and most health insurance, including Medigap, do not pay for long-term care services, in a nursing home or in the community.

*Note: This is different from a nursing home, where short-term skilled care can be covered. We explain that rule below.*

## What Medicare Does Pay For When Someone Lives in Assisted Living

A resident still has medical needs, and Medicare still works for those. These services are billed separately from the facility's monthly fee.

### Doctor Visits and Outpatient Therapy

Part B pays for medically necessary doctor visits and for outpatient physical, occupational and speech therapy. In 2026, you pay a yearly Part B deductible of **$283**, then 20% of the Medicare-approved amount. There is no annual limit on what Medicare pays for medically necessary outpatient therapy.

### Home Health Care

Medicare covers part-time or intermittent skilled care at home. That includes skilled nursing, physical, occupational and speech therapy, and a home health aide, but only while you also receive skilled services. To qualify, you must be homebound and a doctor must order the care. You pay nothing for covered home health services.

Does this work in assisted living? Sometimes. CMS rules say a person can be treated as confined to the home if they live in an assisted living facility that is not mainly providing medical or nursing services. If so, Medicare covers reasonable and necessary home health care there.

There is a catch. Medicare will not pay for home health services that duplicate care the facility must provide under its state license. The same goes for services included in the facility's base contract. CMS explains that choosing to live in such a facility is also a choice to accept the services it offers its residents.

Medicare also does not cover 24-hour care at home, and it does not cover personal care such as bathing or dressing when that is the only care you need.

### Hospice Care

If your loved one has a terminal illness, Medicare-approved hospice care is usually available in the place they live, including an assisted living facility. You pay nothing for covered hospice care, apart from up to $5 per prescription for pain and symptom drugs.

Medicare does not cover room and board while someone receives hospice care in a facility. The assisted living fee still applies.

### Prescription Drugs

Part D is offered to everyone with Medicare through private insurers. It helps pay for brand-name and generic drugs. Enrollment is voluntary.

## Medicare and a Short Rehab Stay Before Assisted Living

Many families first meet Medicare's long-term care rules after a fall or hospital stay. The hospital recommends a rehab stay in a skilled nursing facility (SNF), and later the family starts looking at assisted living.

Medicare Part A can cover that rehab stay if you meet the rules:

- You need a medically necessary inpatient hospital stay of at least **3 days in a row**.
- Observation or emergency room time does not count, even if you stay overnight.
- The care must be skilled care in a Medicare-certified facility.

Here is what you pay in 2026:

| Days in the SNF | Your cost (2026) |
| --------------- | ---------------- |
| **Days 1-20** | $0 after the **$1,736** deductible |
| **Days 21-100** | **$217 per day** |
| **After day 100** | All costs |

Ask the hospital whether your loved one was formally admitted as an inpatient or only placed under observation. The answer decides whether the 3-day rule is met.

This is a rehab benefit. It does not turn into coverage for assisted living when the rehab ends. Once your loved one moves to assisted living, the monthly fee falls back on the family. For what a nursing home stay costs and how Medicare and Medicaid apply there, see our [complete guide to nursing homes](/article/nursing-homes/nursing-homes-complete-guide/).

## Do Medicare Advantage Plans Cover Assisted Living?

Medicare Advantage (Part C) plans are sold by private insurers. They must cover what Original Medicare covers, and most offer extra benefits that Original Medicare does not, such as dental and vision care.

That does not mean a plan pays for assisted living. Extra benefits differ by plan and by year. Some plans have a type called an Institutional Special Needs Plan (I-SNP) for people who live, or are expected to live, in a facility for at least 90 days in a row. The list of qualifying facilities includes nursing facilities and "other" facilities that offer similar long-term health care services.

Before you rely on a plan, do three things:

1. Read the plan's Evidence of Coverage.
2. Call the plan and ask, in those words, whether it pays any part of assisted living.
3. Ask which facilities are in its network.

Get the answer in writing. Do not assume a plan covers it because the brochure lists "long-term care" benefits.

## What Can Help Pay for Assisted Living Instead

Since Medicare does not pay the monthly fee, families usually combine other sources. The median cost of assisted living is **$6,200 per month ($74,400 per year)**, up 5% from 2024 (2025, CareScout/Genworth Cost of Care Survey). See [assisted living costs in your state](/costs/) for local numbers.

- **Medicaid.** Many states offer an assisted living service through Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) waivers. It covers personal care and supportive services, but federal funds are not available for room and board. "Room" covers shelter costs such as rent and utilities, and "board" means three meals a day or a full nutritional regimen. Rules, waitlists and income limits vary by state.
- **PACE.** The Program of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly is for people 55 or older who live in a PACE service area, need a nursing home level of care as certified by their state, and can live safely in the community with PACE support. It covers all Medicare- and Medicaid-covered care. It is available only in some states. Ask the PACE organization whether it can serve your loved one where they live.
- **VA benefits.** Veterans who receive a VA Pension can qualify for Aid and Attendance, an increased monthly amount if they need help with daily functions such as bathing, eating or dressing. Ask the VA how it applies to a specific community. Our [complete guide to assisted living](/article/assisted-living/assisted-living-complete-guide/) lists current rates.
- **Long-term care insurance.** If a policy exists, check whether it covers assisted living. Our article on [whether long-term care insurance is worth it](/article/senior-care-costs/long-term-care-insurance-worth-it/) explains how benefits work.

> If you are not sure what applies, call the Eldercare Locator at **1-800-677-1116**. It is a public service of the Administration for Community Living and can connect you to services near you.

For a full explanation of the care itself, see [What Is Assisted Living?](/article/assisted-living/what-is-assisted-living/)

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does Medicare pay for assisted living after a hospital stay?

No. After a qualifying 3-day inpatient hospital stay, Medicare Part A can pay for up to 100 days in a skilled nursing facility, but not for assisted living. In 2026, you pay $0 for days 1-20 after the $1,736 deductible, and $217 per day for days 21-100. After day 100, you pay all costs.

### Does Medigap cover assisted living?

No. Medicare says that Medicare and most health insurance, including Medigap, do not pay for long-term care services, whether in a nursing home or in the community. You pay all costs for services that are not covered.

### Can a resident get home health care in assisted living?

Sometimes. CMS rules allow Medicare to pay for reasonable and necessary home health care in an assisted living facility that is not mainly providing medical or nursing services. The resident must be homebound, a doctor must order the care, and the services cannot duplicate what the facility must provide under state licensing rules.

### Does Medicare Advantage cover assisted living?

It depends on the plan. Most Medicare Advantage plans offer extra benefits that Original Medicare does not, but benefits vary and a plan may not pay for assisted living at all. Read the Evidence of Coverage and ask the plan in writing before you move.

### Does Medicare cover hospice for someone in assisted living?

Yes. Medicare-approved hospice care is usually available where the person lives, including assisted living. You pay nothing for covered hospice care, except up to $5 per prescription for pain and symptom drugs. Medicare does not pay for room and board, so the facility's monthly fee still applies.
