> **Key Takeaways**
>
> - The national median cost is **$6,200 per month** for assisted living and **$9,581 per month** for a semi-private nursing home room (2025). A private nursing home room costs **$10,798**.
> - At those medians, a semi-private nursing home room costs about **$40,575 more per year** than assisted living (2025, our calculation).
> - Assisted living is for people who need some help with daily tasks but not around-the-clock care. A nursing home is for people who need ongoing medical care (National Institute on Aging).
> - Federal rules require a nursing home to provide licensed nurses on a 24-hour basis and a registered nurse at least **8 hours a day**. Each state sets its own assisted living rules.
> - Medicare pays for a nursing home only for up to **100 days** of skilled care. Medicaid is the primary payer for **63%** of nursing home residents (2025).

---

You are probably weighing two places for someone you love, and neither choice feels easy. Assisted living sounds like a home with help. A nursing home sounds like a hospital. The truth sits somewhere in between, and the right answer depends on the care your loved one needs today and the care they may need next year.

This article compares assisted living vs nursing home in the areas that matter most: what each one costs, what care each one provides, who pays, and how to decide. All cost figures come from the 2025 CareScout Cost of Care Survey, the most recent edition published. Your own price may differ, so use these figures as a yardstick and ask every community or facility for a written fee schedule.

If you want the full picture of either option first, read our [complete guide to assisted living](/article/assisted-living/assisted-living-complete-guide/) or our [complete guide to nursing homes](/article/nursing-homes/nursing-homes-complete-guide/).

## Assisted Living vs Nursing Home: The Short Answer

Assisted living gives a person a place to live with help for daily tasks. A nursing home gives a person a place to live with nursing care at any hour. This table puts the main differences side by side.

| Feature | Assisted living | Nursing home |
| ------- | --------------- | ------------ |
| **Best for** | Some help with daily tasks | Ongoing medical care |
| **Nursing staff** | Set by each state | Licensed nurse 24 hours a day |
| **Rules** | State licensing | State and federal rules |
| **Price basis** | Private one-bedroom | Semi-private or private room |
| **Median cost (2025)** | **$6,200 per month** | **$9,581 per month** (semi-private) |

*Note: Both terms cover a wide range. Some assisted living communities offer more care than others, and nursing homes differ in size and services. Federal nursing staff rules can be waived in limited cases.*

## Assisted Living vs Nursing Home Costs

Cost is often the first question. At the 2025 national median, a nursing home costs more than assisted living in every room type. A median means half of the prices were higher and half were lower.

| Type of care | Monthly | Annual |
| ------------ | ------- | ------ |
| **Assisted living** | $6,200 | $74,400 |
| **Nursing home, semi-private room** | $9,581 | $114,975 |
| **Nursing home, private room** | $10,798 | $129,575 |

### The Gap in Dollars

Here is what a nursing home adds at the national median. These are our calculations from the CareScout medians above.

| Compared with assisted living | Extra per month | Extra per year |
| ----------------------------- | --------------- | -------------- |
| **Semi-private room** | $3,381 | $40,575 |
| **Private room** | $4,598 | $55,175 |

A semi-private room is about **55% higher** than assisted living, and a private room is about **74% higher** (our calculation). Over three years, the semi-private gap grows to about **$121,725**: $344,925 for a nursing home against $223,200 for assisted living. These totals assume prices stay at the 2025 median. Real prices usually rise every year.

### Costs Differ by State

The gap is not the same everywhere. Here is a sample of the 2025 monthly state medians:

| State | Assisted living | Nursing home (semi-private) |
| ----- | --------------- | --------------------------- |
| **Texas** | $5,666 | $5,627 |
| **Missouri** | $5,400 | $6,741 |
| **Illinois** | $6,219 | $8,304 |
| **Ohio** | $6,102 | $9,186 |
| **Florida** | $5,610 | $10,342 |
| **California** | $7,000 | $12,167 |
| **New York** | $7,110 | $15,528 |

Texas is the one state in our data where the assisted living median is slightly higher than the semi-private nursing home median. In New York, the nursing home median is more than double the assisted living median. See our [costs for all 50 states](/costs/) to find your own state.

### Why the Prices Are Not a Perfect Match

A fair comparison needs care. The two prices cover different things.

- **Assisted living:** The base monthly fee usually covers a room, meals, WiFi, 24/7 staff and activities. CareScout says that the more care a resident needs, the more assisted living usually costs. A one-time community fee and yearly rate increases can add to the bill.
- **Nursing home:** The survey's press release does not say which services each daily rate includes. Ask each facility what its rate covers and what costs extra.

For full price details, read our guides to [what assisted living costs per month](/article/assisted-living/how-much-does-assisted-living-cost-per-month/) and [what a nursing home costs per month](/article/nursing-homes/how-much-does-a-nursing-home-cost-per-month/).

## Care Levels: What Each One Provides

The National Institute on Aging describes the split in simple terms. Assisted living is for people who may need some help with daily tasks but do not need around-the-clock care. Nursing homes are for people who cannot care for themselves anymore and may need ongoing medical care.

### Assisted Living: Help With Daily Life

About **1 million** people lived in roughly **32,200** residential care communities in the United States in 2022. These are the communities most families know as assisted living. The most recent CDC figures published (2022) show how much help these residents often need:

- **75%** needed help with bathing.
- **60%** needed help with dressing.
- **44%** had been diagnosed with Alzheimer disease or another dementia.
- **53%** were age 85 or older.

So assisted living is not only for people who are nearly independent. Many residents need real hands-on help every day. What they usually do not need is skilled nursing every day.

Assisted living is mostly a matter of state law. A federal report on residential care says these settings are governed almost exclusively by state laws and regulations, rather than federal rules, and that the rules vary from state to state. That is why a community in one state may offer services that a community in another state cannot.

### Nursing Home: Skilled Nursing at Any Hour

About **1.24 million** people lived in **14,742** federally certified nursing homes in July 2025. Medicaid describes the care in three parts: skilled nursing or medical care, rehabilitation after injury or illness, and long-term care that is needed regularly because of a physical or mental condition.

Federal rules set a floor for nursing staff. A certified nursing home must have enough nursing staff on a 24-hour basis. It must use a registered nurse for at least **8 consecutive hours a day, 7 days a week**, and it must have a full-time registered nurse as director of nursing.

The rules require "sufficient" staff, not a set number of hours. On average, nursing home residents received about **3.85 hours** of nursing care per day in July 2025, including 0.68 hours from registered nurses. A 2024 federal rule that would have set minimum staffing hours was rescinded in December 2025.

### When Assisted Living Is No Longer Enough

Needs change, and assisted living has limits. A federal compendium of state rules (2015 edition) found that many states list conditions that can prevent admission or require a resident to leave. Examples include:

- Needing certain treatments, such as tube feeding, intravenous (IV) therapy or care of an in-dwelling catheter.
- Having certain health conditions, such as a Stage 3 or 4 pressure ulcer.
- Behavior that is a threat to the resident or to others.

*Note: That report is from 2015 and state rules change. Ask any community for its written admission and discharge policy.*

This is why families often start with assisted living and later move to a nursing home. Some communities belong to a larger organization that also offers nursing care, such as a continuing care retirement community. Ask whether that option exists.

## Who Pays: Medicare and Medicaid

Medicare and Medicaid treat these two settings differently.

### Medicare

Medicare does not pay for long-term care, which is mostly help with daily tasks. It pays for skilled nursing facility care only for a limited time. You need a qualifying hospital stay of at least 3 days in a row, and a doctor must decide you need daily skilled care, such as IV treatment or physical therapy. In 2026, days 1-20 cost **$0** after the **$1,736** deductible, and days 21-100 cost **$217 per day**. After day 100, you pay all costs.

Medicare does not pay for the room and meals in assisted living. Our article [Does Medicare Cover Assisted Living?](/article/assisted-living/does-medicare-cover-assisted-living/) explains what it does pay for. For nursing homes, see [Does Medicare Pay for Nursing Home Care?](/article/nursing-homes/does-medicare-pay-for-nursing-home-care/)

### Medicaid

Medicaid treats the two settings very differently:

- **Nursing home:** Nursing facility services are a required Medicaid benefit for people age 21 and older who need them. States may not limit access or use waiting lists for this service. Medicaid is the primary payer for **63%** of nursing home residents (2025).
- **Assisted living:** Many states use Medicaid waiver programs to pay for care services in assisted living. Federal rules do not allow these waivers to pay for room and board. Waivers can also have waiting lists and limited spots.

Medicaid covers a nursing home only if the home is certified for Medicaid, so check before admission. Our article [Medicaid and Nursing Homes](/article/nursing-homes/medicaid-and-nursing-homes/) explains the eligibility tests, and [What Is Medicaid Spend-Down?](/article/senior-care-costs/what-is-medicaid-spend-down/) shows how families reach them.

If money is the hard part, read [What If You Can't Afford Assisted Living?](/article/assisted-living/cant-afford-assisted-living/) Other sources, such as long-term care insurance, may also help in either setting. See [Is Long-Term Care Insurance Worth It?](/article/senior-care-costs/long-term-care-insurance-worth-it/)

## How to Choose Between Assisted Living and a Nursing Home

Start with care needs, not price. A cheaper place that cannot meet your loved one's needs will end in a second move. These signs can help you sort the two options.

**Assisted living may fit when your loved one:**

- Needs help with bathing, dressing, meals or medications.
- Is safe without a nurse watching around the clock.
- Wants a more home-like setting, with their own apartment or room.

**A nursing home may fit when your loved one:**

- Needs daily skilled care, such as IV treatment, wound care or physical therapy.
- Needs ongoing medical care that an assisted living community cannot give.
- Has needs that your state's assisted living rules exclude.

These are guides, not a diagnosis. The best next step is to ask your loved one's doctor to describe the care needed today and what is likely to change.

### Questions to Ask Every Community

The National Institute on Aging suggests asking:

1. How many staff members are there, and how many are trained to provide medical care?
2. Does a doctor check on residents regularly, and how often?
3. What does the fee include, and what services cost extra?
4. Is there a special unit for people with Alzheimer's disease, and does it cost more?
5. Do you accept Medicaid, and what happens if my loved one switches to it?

Visit at different times of day, and talk with other families. For nursing homes, you can compare facilities on Medicare's Care Compare tool.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the main difference between assisted living and a nursing home?

Assisted living helps with daily tasks such as bathing and dressing, and it is for people who do not need around-the-clock care. A nursing home provides skilled nursing at all hours and is for people who need ongoing medical care. The median cost is **$6,200 per month** for assisted living and **$9,581 per month** for a semi-private nursing home room (2025).

### How much cheaper is assisted living than a nursing home?

At the 2025 national medians, assisted living costs about **$3,381 less per month** than a semi-private nursing home room and about **$4,598 less** than a private room (our calculation). Over a year, that is $40,575 and $55,175. The gap varies by state, and in Texas the two medians are almost equal.

### Does Medicare pay for assisted living or a nursing home?

Medicare does not pay for assisted living. It pays for a nursing home only for skilled care after a qualifying hospital stay, for up to **100 days** in a benefit period. In 2026, you pay **$217 per day** for days 21-100, and all costs after day 100.

### Can someone in assisted living move to a nursing home later?

Yes, and many families do. Assisted living has limits, and many states require a resident to leave if they need certain treatments or ongoing skilled nursing. Ask any community for its written discharge policy before you sign, and ask whether it belongs to a group that also offers nursing care.

### Can a person with dementia live in assisted living?

Often yes. About **44%** of residents in residential care communities had been diagnosed with Alzheimer disease or another dementia in 2022. Some communities have special dementia units. Needs change as the disease advances, so read our guide to [memory care](/article/memory-care/memory-care-complete-guide/) as well.
