You may be looking at a price sheet and knowing, with a sinking feeling, that you can't afford assisted living. Or the savings are almost gone and you do not know what comes next. This is more common than most families admit, and there are real options.

No single program pays the whole bill. Families usually combine two or three: a Medicaid waiver for care, income for rent, and sometimes a VA benefit or help from relatives. Each one has rules, and some have waitlists, so it helps to know them before you apply.

This article explains what each source of help covers, who qualifies and what it leaves for you to pay. It also shows what to do first, and what to do if you are turned down. Rules differ by state, so treat this as a map and confirm the details with your state Medicaid agency.

## Can't Afford Assisted Living? Start With the Gap

Assisted living costs a national median of **$6,200 per month**, or **$74,400 per year**, in 2025. For a person whose only income is Supplemental Security Income (SSI), the federal payment is **$994 per month** in 2026. The difference is **$5,206 per month** (our calculation).

Most people pay for assisted living themselves. KFF puts it this way: "most people pay for the costs of assisted living by themselves". The programs below are what is left when that is not possible.

| Option | Who it helps | Pays the rent? |
| ------ | ------------ | -------------- |
| **Medicaid waiver** | Low income, needs a nursing home level of care | No, care services only |
| **VA Aid and Attendance** | Wartime veterans and surviving spouses with a VA pension | No, VA does not pay rent |
| **PACE** | Age 55+, nursing home level of care, in a service area | Ask the PACE plan |
| **State SSI supplement** | SSI recipients in a few states | Varies by state |

Our guide to [assisted living costs per month](/article/assisted-living/how-much-does-assisted-living-cost-per-month/) shows what the monthly fee usually includes. Medicare is not on this list because it does not pay for assisted living. See [Does Medicare Cover Assisted Living?](/article/assisted-living/does-medicare-cover-assisted-living/) for the details.

## Medicaid for Assisted Living: It Pays for Care, Not Rent

Medicaid's role in assisted living is limited. It does not pay the whole monthly fee. Where it helps, it pays for care services.

Here is what the data show:

- **Most states help.** 41 of the 47 responding states cover services in assisted living through at least one Medicaid home care program, and 32 do it through 1915(c) waivers (2024 survey; four states did not respond).
- **Rent is excluded.** Federal waiver guidance says room and board "must be met from participant resources or through other sources." "Room" means shelter costs such as rent and utilities. "Board" means three meals a day.
- **Communities can say no.** Only 10 states require assisted living facilities to accept new residents on Medicaid.

Call each community before you tour. Ask whether it takes Medicaid waiver residents, how many it has now and what it charges them for rent.

### How the Resident Pays the Rent

On a waiver, the resident's own income pays for room and board. The federal guide says the income a waiver participant may keep must cover "a reasonable amount for food, shelter, clothing and other customary living expenses". Social Security, a pension or a state supplement usually goes to the rent.

Some states add money to SSI for people who live in assisted living. Delaware, New Jersey, Rhode Island and Vermont have supplement categories for assisted living or residential care that the Social Security Administration pays for the state. Amounts and rules vary, so ask your state agency whether a supplement exists.

### Who Qualifies for a Medicaid Waiver

A waiver has two tests: financial and functional.

- **Functional.** The person must show a need for the level of care the state requires for an institution.
- **Financial.** Many states use the SSI resource limit of **$2,000** for one person and an income limit of up to **$2,982 per month** (2026), which is 300% of the SSI rate. Your state may use different numbers.

Gifts made in the past five years can delay coverage. Read [What Is Medicaid Spend-Down?](/article/senior-care-costs/what-is-medicaid-spend-down/) before you move any money. For the rules on nursing homes, see [Medicaid and Nursing Homes](/article/nursing-homes/medicaid-and-nursing-homes/).

*Note: Some states use a different program, called a 1915(i) State Plan option, instead of or in addition to a waiver. States set their own criteria for it.*

### The Waitlist Problem

Waivers are not an entitlement. Each state chooses the maximum number of people it will serve. In 2025, about **607,000 people** were on waiting or interest lists for waiver services in 41 states. Waivers for older adults and people with physical disabilities had an average wait of **15 months**, the shortest of any group.

That is why you should apply before the money runs out. Ask the state how to get on the list, and ask whether anyone in a crisis can move up.

## VA Aid and Attendance for Veterans and Surviving Spouses

If your loved one is a wartime veteran or the surviving spouse of one, check the VA. Aid and Attendance is an extra amount added to a VA pension. You must already qualify for the pension, and you need to meet at least one of these conditions:

- You need another person's help with daily activities such as bathing, eating or dressing.
- You stay in bed, or spend much of the day there, because of illness.
- You live in a nursing home because of lost mental or physical abilities.
- Your eyesight is severely limited.

The pension itself needs wartime service, an age of 65 or older (or a disability) and income and net worth within limits. The maximum yearly rates took effect December 1, 2025:

| Who | Maximum per year | Per month |
| --- | ---------------- | --------- |
| **Veteran, no dependents** | $29,093 | $2,424 |
| **Veteran, one dependent** | $34,488 | $2,874 |
| **Surviving spouse** | $18,697 | $1,558 |

*The monthly amounts are our division of VA's annual rates. Your payment is the maximum rate minus your countable income. The net worth limit is **$163,699** through November 30, 2026.*

Even the maximum covers only about **39%** of the $6,200 median fee (our calculation), so most families combine it with other income. VA says it does not pay a veteran's rent in assisted living, though it may pay for some extra services. A VA social worker can explain what applies to your loved one.

State Veterans Homes are another route. They are run by state governments and offer nursing home, domiciliary or adult day health care. Each state sets its own rules, and costs vary.

## PACE: All-Inclusive Care in Some Areas

The Program of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly (PACE) is for people who are 55 or older, live in a PACE service area and need a nursing home level of care certified by the state. They must also be able to live safely in the community with PACE support. PACE covers all Medicare- and Medicaid-covered services plus anything its care team decides you need.

If you have Medicaid, there is no monthly premium. PACE is not available everywhere, and Medicare.gov does not say how it works for assisted living residents. Ask the PACE organization in your area whether it can serve your loved one where they live or plan to live.

## Other Ways to Cover What You Cannot Afford

Programs rarely cover everything. These sources fill the rest:

- **Family contributions.** Relatives can pay part of the fee. Agree on the amount in writing. If your loved one applies for Medicaid, ask the agency how it counts money from relatives.
- **Long-term care insurance.** If a policy exists, check whether it pays for assisted living. Our article on [whether long-term care insurance is worth it](/article/senior-care-costs/long-term-care-insurance-worth-it/) explains how benefits work.
- **Home equity.** A reverse mortgage loan becomes due if the borrower moves out for medical reasons for more than **12 consecutive months**. Selling the home is another route. Ask an elder law attorney before you decide.
- **Tax savings.** You can deduct medical expenses above **7.5% of adjusted gross income** (2025 edition). Meals and lodging in a facility count only if a main reason for being there is medical care.

For dementia care, see [How to Pay for Memory Care](/article/memory-care/how-to-pay-for-memory-care/).

## What to Do When You Can't Afford Assisted Living Right Now

If the money will not last, take these steps in order:

1. **Call the Eldercare Locator at 1-800-677-1116.** It is a public service of the Administration for Community Living and connects you to your local Area Agency on Aging.
2. **Ask your state Medicaid agency about waivers.** Ask for the waitlist process, the asset limit and whether a supplement for SSI exists.
3. **List income, savings, the home and any VA or insurance benefits.** Put the monthly totals on one page.
4. **Look at lower-cost places.** Prices vary a lot by state. Mississippi's median is **$4,369 per month**, against $6,200 nationally (2025). Check [costs in your state](/costs/).
5. **Plan the gap.** Care at home or adult day health care, which has a national median of **$2,058 per month** for 5 days a week (2025), may cost less while you wait. See [what in-home care costs](/article/in-home-care/how-much-does-in-home-care-cost-per-month/).
6. **Ask about respite.** The National Family Caregiver Support Program funds respite and other help for family caregivers through Area Agencies on Aging.

Our [complete guide to assisted living](/article/assisted-living/assisted-living-complete-guide/) covers choosing a community once you know your budget.

## What If the Money Runs Out in a Community

This often happens after a few years of private payment. Before you move in, ask the community in writing what happens when the resident's savings run out.

Policies differ from one community to the next. Ask these questions:

1. Do you accept Medicaid waiver residents?
2. If a resident's money runs out, can they stay?
3. What is the rent for a resident on Medicaid?
4. How many months of private pay do you require first?

Get the answers in writing. If the answer is no, you will know to plan a move before the money runs out.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What happens if you can't afford assisted living?

You have several options. Medicaid waivers can pay for care services in 41 of 47 responding states (2024 survey), VA benefits may help veterans and surviving spouses, and PACE exists in some areas. Call the Eldercare Locator at 1-800-677-1116 to find local programs and to ask about waitlists.

### Does Medicaid pay for assisted living?

Partly. Medicaid can pay for care services in assisted living through waivers in most states, but federal rules do not allow it to pay for room and board. The resident's income covers rent and meals, and only 10 states require communities to accept Medicaid residents.

### How much does VA Aid and Attendance pay for assisted living?

The 2026 maximum is $2,424 per month for a veteran with no dependents and $1,558 for a surviving spouse. Your payment is that maximum minus your countable income. The net worth limit is $163,699 through November 30, 2026.

### How long is the wait for a Medicaid waiver?

It depends on the state. In 2025, waivers for older adults and people with physical disabilities had an average wait of 15 months, and 41 states kept waiting or interest lists. Apply early, before savings run out.

### Can I get free assisted living?

Not usually. Assisted living is rarely free, because Medicaid does not pay for rent and meals. Low-income residents typically pay rent from their own income, and Medicaid pays for care. A state supplement or help from family can fill the gap.
