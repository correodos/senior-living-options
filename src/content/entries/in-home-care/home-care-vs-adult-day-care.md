> **Key Takeaways**
>
> - Home care costs a median of **$35 per hour**, and adult day care a median of **$95 per day** (2025). One adult day costs about as much as **2.7 hours** of home care (our calculation).
> - Home care comes to you and gives one-on-one help. Adult day care gives your loved one company and activities away from home, and gives you a daytime break.
> - Medicare does not pay for adult day care. It pays for part-time home health care only when strict rules are met, and you can still get it if you attend adult day care.
> - Medicaid helps many families: **79%** of adult day center participants had some or all of their services paid by Medicaid (2022).
> - You do not have to choose. Many families use adult day care on some days and home care on others.

---

You may be at the point where your mother or father cannot be alone all day, but a move to a facility feels too soon. Two options keep coming up: someone to help at home, or a daytime program at a center. They sound alike, yet they work very differently, and so do their prices.

This article compares home care and adult day care side by side. You will see what each one offers, what they cost per month at different levels of use, who each one suits, and how Medicare, Medicaid and VA benefits fit in. The cost figures come from the 2025 CareScout Cost of Care Survey, the most recent edition published. There is no single better choice. The right one depends on the care your loved one needs, how much you can pay and what your own days look like.

---

## Home Care vs Adult Day Care: The Short Answer

Choose **home care** when your loved one needs one-on-one help in their own home, or cannot easily leave it. Choose **adult day care** when they are mostly safe at home but need company, activities and some supervision during the day, and you need a break or time to work. Many families combine both.

Here is how they compare:

| Feature | Home care | Adult day care |
| ------- | --------- | -------------- |
| **Where** | In your loved one's home | At a center |
| **Schedule** | Hours you arrange | The center's open hours |
| **Social contact** | Mostly with the caregiver | With other participants |
| **Median cost (2025)** | $35 per hour | $95 per day |
| **Medicare pays** | Only limited skilled care | No |
| **Medicaid may pay** | Yes, through waivers | Yes, in some states |

## What Home Care Includes

Home care means a paid caregiver comes to your loved one's home. CareScout's survey prices a **non-medical caregiver**, who helps with personal care and household tasks. Skilled home health care, such as nursing or therapy, is a separate service with different rules. Our [complete guide to in-home care](/article/in-home-care/in-home-care-complete-guide/) explains both.

Home care can be arranged around your loved one's own routine, because the caregiver works in their home and you choose when the hours happen.

---

## What Adult Day Care Includes

Adult day care is a program at a center that your loved one attends during the day and then returns home. The National Institute on Aging says a center may offer social activities, exercise, meals, personal care and basic health care. The Alzheimer's Association adds that some centers are open from 7 to 10 hours a day, and that transportation and meals are often included.

Adult day centers serve many people. In 2022, an estimated **3,100** centers served about **182,000** participants in the United States. About **61%** of participants needed help with three to six activities of daily living, such as bathing and dressing. So centers serve many people with real care needs, not only people looking for company.

*Note: Services differ a lot from one center to the next. Few centers offer everything, so ask what is included before you enroll.*

---

## Home Care vs Adult Day Care Cost

Adult day care is usually the lower-cost option. The 2025 CareScout survey reports a national median of **$95 per day** for adult day health care and **$35 per hour** for a non-medical home caregiver. The adult day rate was down 5% from 2024, and the home care rate was up 3%.

Divide $95 by $35 and you get about **2.7 hours**. So a full day at a center costs about the same as under three hours of home care (our calculation).

### Monthly Cost at Different Levels of Use

These are our calculations from the 2025 medians. Both use 52 weeks a year divided by 12 months:

| Care | How often | Monthly cost |
| ---- | --------- | ------------ |
| **Adult day care** | 2 days a week | $823 |
| **Adult day care** | 3 days a week | $1,235 |
| **Adult day care** | 5 days a week | $2,058 |
| **Home care** | 10 hours a week | $1,517 |
| **Home care** | 20 hours a week | $3,033 |
| **Home care** | 44 hours a week | $6,673 |

The $2,058 and $6,673 figures are the survey's own monthly medians: adult day care at 5 days a week, and home care at 44 hours a week. The other rows are estimates.

The comparison is not exact. A day at a center covers many hours, plus activities and often meals. Ten hours of home care is a few hours of one-on-one help. What matters is the amount of care your loved one needs.

### Costs by State

Prices vary widely. In 2025, the state median for adult day care ran from **$1,246 per month** in Alabama to **$5,783** in Montana (5 days a week). A few states did not have enough data to report a figure.

## Who Each Option Suits

**Home care may suit your loved one when:**

- They need hands-on help with bathing, dressing or toileting.
- Leaving the house is hard, or a center would be too tiring.
- They do best with a familiar place and routine.

**Adult day care may suit your loved one when:**

- They are lonely or have little contact with other people.
- They need supervision during the day but are comfortable around others.
- You work, or you need regular time to rest. The Alzheimer's Association says centers give caregivers a much-needed break and let working caregivers balance a job with caregiving.

### What About Dementia?

Adult day centers serve people with Alzheimer's and other dementias, with structured activities such as music and exercise. The person will need time to adjust, and many come to enjoy it after a few weeks. The Alzheimer's Association suggests trying a center at least twice a week for a month. Ask whether staff are trained in dementia, how emergencies are handled and whether people with dementia join the general activities or are kept apart.

## Using Both Home Care and Adult Day Care

Many families use both. Adult day care can cover weekdays, and a home caregiver can help in the mornings, evenings or on weekends. The two programs do not block each other:

- **Medicare:** "You can still get home health care if you attend adult day care."
- **VA:** Adult Day Health Care can be combined with other home and community services.

For more ways to get a break, see our [caregiver resources guide](/article/caregiver-resources/caregiver-resources-complete-guide/).

## Who Pays for Home Care and Adult Day Care

**Medicare.** Medicare does not pay for adult day care. For home care, it covers skilled nursing and home health aide services only if you are homebound and need skilled care. It does not cover personal care when that is the only care you need, or 24-hour care at home.

**Medicaid.** Home and Community-Based Services waivers can pay for services such as personal care, adult day health and respite care, depending on the state. In 2022, about **79%** of adult day center participants had some or all of their services paid by Medicaid. Our article on [Medicaid spend-down](/article/senior-care-costs/what-is-medicaid-spend-down/) explains the financial rules.

**VA.** Enrolled veterans who meet the clinical criteria may get Adult Day Health Care or a Homemaker and Home Health Aide, and a copay may apply.

**PACE.** In some states, the Program of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly (PACE) covers adult day primary care for people who are at least 55 and need a nursing home level of care.

**Other help.** Many adult day centers use sliding-scale fees, and some long-term care insurance plans pay part of the cost.

## Questions to Ask Before You Decide

For an adult day center, ask:

1. What services are included, and what costs extra?
2. Is transportation available?
3. What are the minimum attendance and absence rules?
4. Are staff trained to care for people with dementia?
5. Can we visit and try a few days first?

For home care, see the checklist in our [in-home care cost guide](/article/in-home-care/how-much-does-in-home-care-cost-per-month/).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is adult day care cheaper than home care?

Usually. The 2025 national median for adult day care is **$95 per day**, about the same as 2.7 hours of home care at **$35 per hour** (our calculation). At 5 days a week, adult day care costs a median of **$2,058 per month**, compared with **$6,673** for home care at 44 hours a week.

### Does Medicare pay for adult day care?

No. The National Institute on Aging says Medicare will not pay for adult day care, but Medicaid and other government programs may. The Program of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly (PACE) is the exception in some states, and it covers adult day primary care for people who qualify.

### How many days a week should someone go to adult day care?

There is no single answer. For a person with dementia, the Alzheimer's Association suggests trying a center at least twice a week for a month, because the person needs time to adjust. At the 2025 median of $95 per day, two days a week costs about **$823 per month** (our calculation).

### Can a person with dementia go to adult day care?

Yes. Adult day centers serve people with Alzheimer's and other dementias with structured activities such as music and exercise. Before you choose a center, ask whether staff are trained in dementia and how emergencies are handled.

### Can you use home care and adult day care together?

Yes. Medicare says you can still get home health care if you attend adult day care. VA also lets Adult Day Health Care be combined with other home and community services. Many families use a center on weekdays and a home caregiver at other times.
