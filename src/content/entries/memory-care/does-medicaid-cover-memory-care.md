> **Key Takeaways**
>
> - Medicaid can pay for memory care, but how much depends on the setting. In a nursing home it pays for care and room. In assisted living it pays for care services only, not rent or meals.
> - Memory care is not a Medicaid category. The federal nursing home and waiver pages do not mention dementia units, so coverage depends on how the community is licensed and what your state pays for.
> - Only **10 states** require assisted living communities to accept new Medicaid residents (KFF, 2024 survey). Most, but not all, nursing homes accept Medicaid.
> - Many states use a **$2,000** asset limit and an income limit of up to **$2,982 per month** (2026). Gifts made in the past **60 months** can delay coverage.
> - Waivers for older adults and people with physical disabilities had an average wait of **15 months** (2025). Set up a power of attorney early.

A doctor has said the words "memory care," and now you are asking whether Medicaid will help pay. It is the right question, and the answer is "partly." Families often learn the details only after they have chosen a community, and by then the bills have started.

This article explains what Medicaid pays for in each place a person with dementia may live, and what it does not. It also covers seven things families often miss: from the rent that stays with the resident to the paperwork you need before it is too late. Rules differ by state, so use this as a map and confirm the details with your state Medicaid agency.

Our [complete guide to memory care](/article/memory-care/memory-care-complete-guide/) explains the care itself. For the full list of payment options, see [How to Pay for Memory Care](/article/memory-care/how-to-pay-for-memory-care/).

## Does Medicaid Cover Memory Care? It Depends on the Setting

Memory care is a type of care, not a Medicaid program. People with dementia live in several kinds of places, and Medicaid treats each one differently. About **44%** of residents of residential care communities, which include assisted living, had been diagnosed with Alzheimer's disease or another dementia in 2022.

| Setting | What Medicaid pays | What you pay |
| ------- | ------------------ | ------------ |
| **Nursing home** | Nursing facility care, including room | Most of your income, after allowances |
| **Assisted living** | Care services, in states that cover them | Rent and meals |

The Alzheimer's Association says memory care units "can take many forms and exist within various types of residential care communities, including assisted living," and that they "may or may not be locked or secured". It also says definitions of assisted living vary from state to state. So the label "memory care" does not tell you what Medicaid will pay.

## Medicaid and Memory Care in a Nursing Home

Nursing home care is where Medicaid does the most. A nursing facility that takes part in Medicaid must provide or arrange nursing and related services. If a person with dementia needs that level of care, Medicaid can pay once the financial rules are met.

Three points matter for families:

- **The state defines the level of care.** "Need for nursing facility services is defined by states," and the federal page does not mention dementia. Ask your state how it assesses memory loss, wandering and supervision needs.
- **Not every home accepts Medicaid.** "Most, but not all, nursing homes accept Medicaid payment". If the home has a dementia unit, ask whether the beds in that unit are Medicaid-certified.
- **The resident still pays from income.** Medicaid pays after the resident's income goes toward the bill. The resident keeps a small personal needs allowance. Our article [Medicaid and Nursing Homes](/article/nursing-homes/medicaid-and-nursing-homes/) explains the three eligibility tests and how much the resident keeps.

For comparison, the national median cost of a nursing home is **$9,581 per month** for a semi-private room and **$10,798** for a private room (2025, CareScout; our monthly calculation from the survey's daily rates). See [what a nursing home costs per month](/article/nursing-homes/how-much-does-a-nursing-home-cost-per-month/).

## Medicaid and Memory Care in Assisted Living

Many memory care units are inside assisted living communities, and this is where families are surprised. Medicaid does not pay the whole fee.

In KFF's 2024 survey, **41 of 47** responding states covered services in assisted living through at least one Medicaid home care program, and 32 did it through 1915(c) waivers. Four states did not respond. A waiver can pay for services such as personal care. It cannot pay for room and board. The federal guide defines "room" as shelter costs such as rent and utilities, and "board" as three meals a day.

CareScout does not publish a separate national median for memory care, so no official price exists to compare against. The national median for standard assisted living is **$6,200 per month** (2025). Our article on [assisted living costs](/article/assisted-living/how-much-does-assisted-living-cost-per-month/) shows what the fee covers. Ask each memory care community for its monthly rate and any added unit fee in writing. The Alzheimer's Association notes that some states require communities to disclose these fees.

## The Financial Rules in 2026

The rules below are the usual starting point. Your state may use different numbers.

| Rule | 2026 figure |
| ---- | ----------- |
| **Asset limit, one person** | $2,000 in many states |
| **Income limit, special income level** | Up to $2,982 per month |
| **Look-back for gifts** | 60 months |
| **Home equity limit** | $752,000 to $1,130,000 |
| **Spouse at home keeps (resources)** | $32,532 to $162,660 |
| **Spouse at home keeps (income)** | At least $2,705 per month |

The asset and income numbers come from CMS. The $2,982 figure is 300% of the $994 SSI rate. Each state picks its home equity limit within the range shown. From January 1, 2028, a new federal law caps the top of that range at $1,000,000 for homes not on agricultural land.

## What Families Often Miss

### Memory Care Is Not a Medicaid Category

Federal Medicaid pages describe nursing facility services and waiver services. They do not set up a "memory care benefit." What Medicaid pays for in a memory care unit depends on how the building is licensed and which state program covers it. Ask the community which Medicaid program it takes, and ask your state which programs cover dementia care.

### The Community May Say No

Only 10 states require assisted living communities to accept new residents who are on Medicaid. A community can be a great fit and still not take Medicaid. Call before you tour.

### Rent and Meals Stay With the Resident

On a waiver, the resident's income covers room and board. The federal guide says a waiver participant's allowance must include "a reasonable amount for food, shelter, clothing and other customary living expenses." If your loved one's income is smaller than the community's rent, ask what rent it charges Medicaid residents.

### The Waitlist Is Real

Waivers are capped. In 2025, about **607,000 people** were on waiting or interest lists in 41 states. Waivers for older adults and people with physical disabilities had an average wait of **15 months**. Apply before the money runs out.

### Gifts Can Delay Coverage

Medicaid reviews the 60 months before you apply. The Alzheimer's Association warns that giving assets to family members to qualify is "risky" because strict laws govern it. Read [What Is Medicaid Spend-Down?](/article/senior-care-costs/what-is-medicaid-spend-down/) before you move any money.

### The Clock on Back Pay Is Shrinking

Today, Medicaid can pay for care received up to **3 months** before the month of application, if the person would have been eligible. For applications made on or after **January 1, 2027**, that falls to **2 months** for most people.

### Paperwork Is Hard When Memory Is Failing

Someone has to sign the application. States must accept an application from an authorized representative, or from someone acting responsibly for an applicant who is incapacitated. The Alzheimer's Association advises putting a durable power of attorney in place "while he or she is still able to make decisions". Without one, a court may have to appoint a guardian, which takes time and involves an attorney.

### What Happens After Death

States must seek repayment from the estate of anyone who was 55 or older when they received Medicaid long-term care, with limits. The state cannot recover while a surviving spouse is alive, and states must have a hardship process. This is called estate recovery, and rules differ by state.

## How to Apply and What to Ask

Start before the savings are gone:

1. **Call your state Medicaid agency.** Ask which programs cover dementia care, the waitlist process and the asset limit.
2. **Call the Eldercare Locator at 1-800-677-1116.** It connects you to your local Area Agency on Aging.
3. **Name an agent.** Put a durable power of attorney in place while your loved one can still sign.
4. **Gather five years of statements.** Keep bank records for the full 60-month look-back.
5. **Ask each community in writing:** Do you accept Medicaid? Does your memory care unit? What is the rent for a Medicaid resident? Can a resident stay when private money runs out?

If Medicaid is not yet available, our article [What If You Can't Afford Assisted Living?](/article/assisted-living/cant-afford-assisted-living/) covers VA benefits, PACE and other help.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does Medicaid pay for memory care in assisted living?

Partly. In most states Medicaid can pay for care services in assisted living through waivers, but federal rules do not allow it to pay for room and board. The resident's income covers rent and meals, and only 10 states require communities to accept Medicaid residents.

### Does Medicaid pay for dementia care in a nursing home?

Yes, for people who need that level of care and meet the financial rules. Medicaid covers nursing facility care, and most nursing homes accept it. Ask whether the dementia unit's beds are Medicaid-certified.

### Can a memory care facility refuse Medicaid?

Yes. Only 10 states require assisted living communities to accept new Medicaid residents, and most, but not all, nursing homes accept Medicaid. Ask before you tour whether the community takes Medicaid residents and what rent it charges them.

### Can I apply for Medicaid if my parent has dementia and cannot sign?

Often yes. States must accept an application from an authorized representative or from someone acting responsibly for an incapacitated applicant. A durable power of attorney set up early makes this easier, and without one a court may need to appoint a guardian.

### Will Medicaid take my parent's house?

The home generally does not count toward the asset limit, but equity above your state's limit, between $752,000 and $1,130,000 in 2026, can block coverage. After death the state may seek repayment from the estate, but not while a surviving spouse is alive. Some states can also place a lien on the home of a permanent nursing home resident.
