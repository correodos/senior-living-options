> **Key Takeaways**
>
> - The CareScout survey does not publish a price for memory care. The closest benchmarks are assisted living at **$6,200 per month** and nursing home care at **$9,581 to $10,798 per month** (2025).
> - Medicare does not pay for custodial care. It covers up to **100 days** of skilled nursing after a hospital stay, with **$217 per day** from day 21 (2026).
> - Medicaid can pay for care in a memory care setting, but federal law keeps it from paying room and board in community settings. Nursing home Medicaid is required in every state.
> - A veteran with no dependents can receive up to **$2,424 per month** through VA Aid and Attendance, and a surviving spouse up to **$1,558** (2026).
> - Long-term care insurance is usually not available once Alzheimer's symptoms appear, so check any policy that already exists.

---

You are probably holding a price sheet for memory care and wondering how anyone pays for it. It is a fair worry. The monthly fee is high, it keeps going, and no single program covers all of it. Most families end up combining several sources.

This article walks through every main option: your own income and savings, Medicare, Medicaid, VA benefits, long-term care insurance, home equity and tax savings. For each one you will see what it covers, what it does not, and the 2026 figures that matter. The rules come from official sources, with KFF and the Alzheimer's Association as support. Every state differs a little, so treat this as a map and confirm the details with your state agency or an elder law attorney.

## How to Pay for Memory Care: Start With the Real Price

The 2025 CareScout Cost of Care Survey does not report memory care as its own category. So there is no official national median. These are the closest 2025 benchmarks:

| Type of care | Monthly median (2025) |
| ------------ | --------------------- |
| **Assisted living, private one-bedroom** | $6,200 |
| **Nursing home, semi-private room** | $9,581 |
| **Nursing home, private room** | $10,798 |

Memory care prices differ by community and by the care level your loved one needs. Ask each one for a written fee schedule before you plan the budget. Our [complete guide to memory care](/article/memory-care/memory-care-complete-guide/) explains what drives the price.

### Who Usually Pays

The most recent edition of a federal research brief (2022) projects how long-term care is paid for by adults who turn 65 between 2021 and 2025. It counts "community-based" care, which includes residential care such as assisted living, separately from nursing facility care:

| Payer | Community-based, including residential care | Nursing facility |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------- | ---------------- |
| **Family, out of pocket** | 44% | 31% |
| **Medicaid** | 33% | 52% |
| **Private insurance** | 8% | 2% |

These are projections for the lifetime costs of people turning 65, not a survey of memory care residents. They show a clear pattern: families carry the largest share of residential care costs, and Medicaid carries more of the nursing home bill.

## Medicare: Short-Term Care Only

Medicare Part A pays for skilled nursing facility care for up to **100 days** per benefit period. You need a prior inpatient hospital stay of at least 3 days in a row. In 2026, days 1-20 cost **$0** after the **$1,736** deductible, days 21-100 cost **$217 per day**, and after day 100 you pay all costs.

Medicare says it does not pay for long-term care, and that you pay all costs for non-covered services, including most long-term care. That means it does not pay the monthly fee of a memory care community. Our article [Does Medicare Pay for Nursing Home Care?](/article/nursing-homes/does-medicare-pay-for-nursing-home-care/) explains the short-term rules.

### The GUIDE Program

The CMS GUIDE model supports people with dementia and their caregivers. Participating providers give patients and caregivers a care navigator, and CMS reimburses caregiver respite services of up to **$2,500 per year** per eligible patient. The model began on July 1, 2024 and runs for 8 years. The respite allowance helps a family caregiver rest. At $2,500 a year, it is far smaller than a memory care fee.

## How to Pay for Memory Care With Medicaid

Medicaid is the largest payer of long-term care, at a projected 43% of costs for people turning 65. It works differently depending on where your loved one lives.

| Feature | Nursing home Medicaid | HCBS waiver |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Required in every state** | Yes | No, each state chooses |
| **Waiting list allowed** | No | States set a maximum number served |
| **What it pays for** | Nursing facility care | Services, not room and board |

### Nursing Home Medicaid

States must cover nursing facility services for adults 21 and older who need them, and they may not put access on a waiting list. If your loved one needs a nursing home level of care, Medicaid can pay for it once they meet the financial rules. Some memory care units are part of nursing homes, so ask whether the unit accepts Medicaid.

### HCBS Waivers for Assisted Living and Memory Care

Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) waivers let a state pay for care in a home or community setting. Applicants must need the level of care a state requires for an institution. The federal law behind the waiver allows payment for services "other than room and board," with narrow exceptions such as short respite stays.

In practice, the waiver may pay for personal care in a memory care community, while the resident pays the room and board from Social Security, a pension or savings. A 2025 KFF analysis found that 41 of 47 responding states cover services in assisted living through at least one program, and 32 do it through 1915(c) waivers. Because each state sets its own enrollment limit, you may face a waitlist.

### The Financial Rules

In many states, the asset limit for a single applicant is **$2,000** and the income cap is **$2,982 per month** (2026). Some states use different numbers. Medicaid also looks back **60 months** (5 years) at gifts and transfers. Our article on [Medicaid spend-down](/article/senior-care-costs/what-is-medicaid-spend-down/) explains how it works, and your state's page in our [costs by state guide](/costs/) shows local rules. Apply early, and talk to an elder law attorney before moving any money.

## VA Benefits for Veterans and Surviving Spouses

### Aid and Attendance

Aid and Attendance is an extra amount added to a VA pension. You must already receive a pension and meet one of these conditions: you need another person's help with daily activities, you stay in bed much of the day because of illness, or you are in a nursing home because of lost mental or physical abilities. A veteran must also meet wartime service and net worth rules.

The 2026 maximum annual rates, effective December 1, 2025, divide into these monthly amounts:

| Who | Maximum per year | Maximum per month |
| --- | ---------------- | ----------------- |
| **Veteran, no dependents** | $29,093 | $2,424 |
| **Veteran, one dependent** | $34,488 | $2,874 |
| **Surviving spouse** | $18,697 | $1,558 |

The surviving spouse rate comes from VA's survivors pension page. Your actual payment is the maximum rate minus your countable income. The net worth limit is **$163,699** from December 1, 2025 to November 30, 2026.

VA reviews asset transfers made in the **3 years** before a pension claim. A transfer below fair market value can lead to a penalty of up to 5 years without benefits.

*Note: VA does not pay the rent in an assisted living facility, but it may pay for some extra services. Aid and Attendance is separate: it is an add-on to a VA pension.*

### State Veterans Homes

State Veterans Homes are owned and run by state governments, not by VA. They provide nursing home, domiciliary or adult day health care. Each state sets its own eligibility rules, the cost varies, and VA may pay toward a veteran's care. There are homes in all 50 states and Puerto Rico. Ask any home whether it has a dementia unit.

## Long-Term Care Insurance

A long-term care insurance policy may pay part of the monthly fee, up to its daily or monthly limit. The Alzheimer's Association says this coverage is usually not available for purchase after symptoms of Alzheimer's appear.

If your loved one already has a policy, call the insurer now. Ask what triggers benefits, how long the waiting period lasts, how much it pays per day and what care settings it covers. Our article on [whether long-term care insurance is worth it](/article/senior-care-costs/long-term-care-insurance-worth-it/) explains how benefits work.

## Home Equity and Reverse Mortgages

Some families use the value of a home to pay for care. If your loved one has a reverse mortgage, known as a Home Equity Conversion Mortgage (HECM), learn the move-out rule first. The loan comes due when the borrower no longer lives in the home, including moving out for medical reasons for more than **12 consecutive months**. A co-borrower can stay in the home. A spouse who is not a co-borrower may stay only if they qualify under HUD's rules.

So a reverse mortgage may have to be repaid if your loved one lives in memory care for more than 12 consecutive months and no co-borrower stays in the home. Ask the loan servicer what applies, and see an elder law attorney before you sell or borrow against the home.

## Tax Savings

Memory care costs may lower your taxes. The IRS lets you include the cost of medical care in a nursing home, home for the aged or similar institution as a medical expense. Meals and lodging count only if a principal reason for being there is medical care. For qualified long-term care services, a licensed practitioner must certify that the person cannot do at least two daily activities without substantial help, or needs substantial supervision because of severe cognitive impairment. You can deduct only the part of your total medical expenses above **7.5% of adjusted gross income** (2025 edition). Ask a tax professional how it applies to you.

## Putting the Pieces Together

Few families rely on a single source. The federal brief notes that people with longer spells of need may pay out of pocket until they qualify for Medicaid. Take these steps in order:

1. Ask the community for a written fee schedule and what triggers extra charges.
2. List your loved one's income, savings, home and any policy or VA eligibility.
3. Call the Eldercare Locator at **1-800-677-1116** to find local help for older adults and their families.
4. Apply early if you expect to need Medicaid or a waiver, because states may cap enrollment.
5. See an elder law attorney before you give, sell or move assets.

> The Alzheimer's Association Helpline is open 24/7 at **1-800-272-3900**.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do most families pay for memory care?

Families pay the largest share themselves. A federal projection shows them paying **44%** of community-based care costs out of pocket, with Medicaid paying **33%**, for adults turning 65 between 2021 and 2025. Medicaid pays more when the care is in a nursing facility, at **52%**.

### Can Medicaid pay for memory care room and board?

No. Federal Medicaid rules allow waiver payments for services other than room and board, with narrow exceptions such as short respite stays. The resident usually pays room and board from income or savings. Nursing home Medicaid is different because it covers the nursing home stay.

### Does the VA pay for memory care?

VA does not pay the rent in an assisted living facility. But Aid and Attendance can add up to **$2,424 per month** to a veteran's pension (2026), and up to **$1,558** for a surviving spouse. Your payment depends on your income and a net worth limit of **$163,699**.

### Can I use a reverse mortgage to pay for memory care?

It is risky. A HECM comes due when the borrower moves out for more than **12 consecutive months** for medical reasons, unless a co-borrower still lives in the home. Ask the lender what applies to your loan before you decide.

### Can I buy long-term care insurance after a dementia diagnosis?

Usually not. The Alzheimer's Association says long-term care insurance is generally unavailable once symptoms of Alzheimer's appear. If a policy already exists, check its benefit triggers and waiting period.

### Is memory care tax deductible?

Part of it may be. The IRS lets you include nursing home or similar institution medical care as a medical expense, but only the amount above **7.5% of adjusted gross income** is deductible. A tax professional can confirm what applies to you.
